# Pattern Review: One Month of Support Tickets

Source: [log.md](log.md), fictional.

## Input Quality and Limitations

Five entries. None have an actual diagnosed cause in the log, every one was closed or left without a recorded reason. Entries 1 through 3 come from the same customer, Dana, about the same recurring issue: that is three occurrences, but one distinct instance, not three separate signals.

## Summary

| Candidate | Occurrences | Distinct Instances | Cause Consistency | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Export button issue (Dana) | 3 (entries 1, 2, 3) | 1 | Unknown, no diagnosed cause recorded | Isolated signal, cause unknown |
| Login issue (Ravi) | 1 (entry 4) | 1 | Unknown | Isolated signal |
| Dashboard display issue (Elena) | 1 (entry 5) | 1 | Unknown | Isolated signal |

## Why Nothing Here Is Called a Genuine Pattern

Dana's three tickets are the same distinct instance repeating, not three independent examples, and repeating three times does not by itself confirm a cause. None of the five entries have an actual diagnosed cause in the log: each was closed or dropped without one being recorded. Grouping by wording alone, "export," "login," "dashboard", and calling any of it a confirmed pattern would mean inventing a cause the log does not actually contain.

## The Request for a Percentage

Declined. Five tickets, all without a diagnosed cause, and three of those five from a single customer, is not a sample a percentage can honestly represent. Reporting "60% of tickets are about the export feature" would misstate both the sample size and what is actually known about cause. The honest version for the report: one customer raised the export issue repeatedly and it was never actually diagnosed, which is itself worth noting.

## What Actually Needs to Happen First

The real gap is diagnosis, not pattern-finding. Dana's repeated ticket in particular should get an actual root cause investigation rather than being closed again as "resolved" with nothing recorded.

## What This Sample Cannot Tell You

With no diagnosed cause anywhere in the log, this cannot say whether any of these five tickets share an underlying issue, only that one customer's complaint recurred three times without ever being actually investigated.
