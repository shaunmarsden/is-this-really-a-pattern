# Pattern Review: Team Retrospective Feedback

Source: [log.md](log.md), four sprints, fictional.

## Input Quality and Limitations

Four entries across four sprints, each from a different team member, so instance identity is clear. Three of four entries have a diagnosed cause with evidence in the log; entry 4 is a one-off with no recurrence.

## Summary

| Candidate | Occurrences | Distinct Instances | Cause Consistency | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blocked reviews with no escalation | 2 (entries 1, 3) | 2 | Same cause both times | Medium |
| Meeting load reducing focus time | 1 (entry 2) | 1 | N/A, single instance | Isolated signal |

## Genuine Pattern

**Blocked reviews with no escalation process (entries 1 and 3).** Both Alex and Sam used different wording, "communication was frustrating" versus "felt like nobody knew what I was doing", but the diagnosed cause in both cases is the same: an assigned reviewer became unavailable and nothing reassigned or escalated the blocked review. Two distinct instances, same cause, confidence medium given the small sample size. This is worth a real process fix: a rule for reassigning or flagging a review after a set number of days with no movement.

## Misleading Surface Pattern

**"Communication" as a shared word does not mean a shared cause.** Entries 1 and 2 both use the word "communication," which could read as one pattern at a glance. The diagnosed causes are genuinely different: entry 1 is about an unescalated blocker, entry 2 is about meeting load specifically. Treating these as the same issue would misdirect any fix, an escalation rule does nothing for meeting overload, and fewer meetings does nothing for an unescalated blocked review.

## Isolated Signals, Not Yet Patterns

- Priya's meeting-load concern (entry 2): only one instance so far, worth watching, not yet a pattern
- Jordan's local environment issue (entry 4): a one-off, resolved same day, no recurrence noted

## Suggested Actions for Human Approval

- Consider a reassignment or escalation rule for reviews blocked more than a set number of days, based on the confirmed pattern in entries 1 and 3
- Watch for further meeting-load feedback before treating entry 2 as more than an isolated signal

## What This Sample Cannot Tell You

Four entries across four sprints is a small sample. This does not tell you how common blocked reviews are across the whole team's history, only that this specific cause recurred twice with the same underlying reason in the entries actually logged.
